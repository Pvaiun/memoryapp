Memory · Day view · Going late

Something going late, while the day is still on

The map is composed once at the 5am rebuild and then holds still. Prominence drift is off the table — the map earns its keep by being a shape you can remember, and re-ranking cards mid-afternoon destroys exactly that. So whatever lateness looks like, it has to happen in place: same card, same position, same silhouette.

Five treatments below, all driven by one clock. Drag it through the sleep day and watch the same two real cards from today's map respond. The last section covers what changes once dates can be timeless.

The precedent

The map already moves during the day — but only downward

Worth naming before choosing anything: DescentView already re-depths cards mid-day. A bubble whose items are all resolved gives up its Brain prominence and sinks to p 0 — "resolution is depth (user decision)". It also distinguishes how it got there: settledWord reads "done" when you worked it and "passed" when the clock alone settled it.

So the corridor already has a vocabulary for a moment going by — it just spends it entirely on things leaving. A HAPPEN that passes goes quiet and sinks. A DO whose moment passes does nothing at all. That asymmetry is the gap, and it argues for a treatment that's the opposite gesture to settling, not a louder version of urgency.

Control · what ships today

Today the card does change — for the wrong reason, at the wrong time

Date-only deadlines are stored at local noon (localNoonIso). bubbleStatus compares that instant to Date.now(), so Home chores flips to red overdue at 12:01pm — from the storage convention, not from anything you did or didn't do.

Meanwhile deriveDeadlineNotch counts in whole sleep-days, so the notch still says TODAY. Two bricks on one card, disagreeing, every afternoon.

And the one item on that card with a real moment — the dishwasher at midnight — is still eleven hours away when the card goes red.

Option A

The notch flips

The corner brick already exists and already carries time pressure. It just gains a past tense: 7PM → SINCE 7PM. One string, one colour token, no new element.

Silhouette is untouched — the notch is already there, already that size. But it's the smallest type on the card at 9px, and it's the first thing depth crops. At corridor distance you'd never see it.

Option B

The chip that went by goes hollow

Lateness lands on the individual chip, not the card. The chip loses its accent fill and takes a dashed edge. Everything else is untouched.

It deliberately adds no words. At the real 288px card width the prose already wraps hard around inline-flex chips, so any appended "· went by" reflows the sentence and the card changes height — which is the shape problem again, in miniature. Restyling in place is the only move that leaves the layout alone.

This is also the honest one. A card is routinely half late — the 11am check-in went by, the 7pm French hasn't yet — and every card-level treatment has to lie about that. It puts the mark exactly where the tap already goes.

Cost: it reads at arm's length, not across the corridor, and a chip going hollow is a subtraction, which is quiet by design. Drag past 11am and 7pm to see them go one at a time.

Option C

A day rail drains along the top edge

Reuses the span rail's slot and visual language exactly — the 4px track hugging the card's top edge — but the axis is today, 5am to 5am, and the ticks are the card's own moments. The fill advances; a moment you passed unticked lights up behind it.

It's the only option that shows how long ago, which is the thing that actually differs between "7:05pm" and "11:40pm". It's also the only one that adds a component and puts a slowly-moving element on every card — a small but real tax on the still-life quality.

Option D

The rim takes a second colour

The theme rim is one of exactly three homes for the accent, it sits on every card, and it survives depth better than anything else on the card. Give it a second segment that grows as moments go by.

Best glanceability of the five — you'd read it from the top of the corridor. But it spends the rim, which is currently pure identity: this card is Relationship. Making it also carry state means the one element you use to recognize a card at distance now changes during the day. That's the closest thing here to the drift problem, just in colour instead of position.

Option E

Nothing on the card — the header counts it

Leave all thirty cards exactly as composed and put the whole concept in the header, next to the date line, where the descent already tracks urgentCount. The ledger — already built, already lists every card with its status word — gains went by alongside done and overdue.

Perfect shape preservation: it is literally impossible for this to change a card. And the count is arguably the true unit — you don't need to know which card at a glance, you need to know there's something to go back for.

Cost: it's a number, not a place. You have to go looking.

The other half

Timeless dates shrink the problem to a third of its size

Every mock above is driven by real moments — 11am, 7pm, midnight. Today's map has fourteen dated items and only five carry a genuine clock time. The rest are noon-anchored fictions.

Once a date can be day-precision, a timeless item has no moment to miss during the day. It becomes late at the sleep-day boundary — 5am — which is exactly when the map rebuilds anyway. So its lateness is a build-time fact, composed into the card's prose by the Brain, and never a within-day transition at all.

That's the real prize: the within-day display problem stops applying to cat litters and the sink, and applies only to things you actually said a time for.

The 4am problem

The sleep-day boundary is for state, never for speech

If a timeless item goes late at 5am, the naive move is to notify then. That's useless, and the current runway model already produces a related absurdity in the other direction. The rule that resolves both: day-precision items never take the punctual push path. They get one evening sweep, at a waking hour, and then silence until the map speaks in the morning.

Where I'd land

Do the timeless work first, and possibly only that. It deletes the noon lie, fixes the notch/chip contradiction, and removes two-thirds of the within-day cases by making them build-time cases instead — where the Brain can say them in prose, which is a far better instrument than any badge. It's the change that makes the display question small enough to answer well.

For what's left — genuinely timed things going by — B is the honest answer and D is the visible one. They're not exclusive: the rim carries "something on this card went by" at distance, the chip says which one when you arrive. That pairing costs no silhouette and no position.

I'd rule out A (invisible where it matters) and C (a moving part on every card, against a map whose value is stillness). E is worth keeping as an addition whichever way you go — the ledger already has the slot.

Build order
Day-precision dates end to end (schema, parser, display, push) → then B alone → then D only if B proves too quiet in practice. Each step is independently shippable and none of them move a card.

Cards, chips, notches, pips and rim geometry are copied from src/app/styles.css; accents are the real themeColor() hashes for Relationship and Home. The lateness token is deliberately not --danger — being late is not an emergency, and the app already spends red on due-now and happening-now.
